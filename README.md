Multi-modal cell microscopy image segmentation is a core component of High-Content Imaging and Analysis (HCIA), and its segmentation accuracy directly impacts the reliability of HCIA analysis results. Although deep learning-based cell segmentation methods have been widely applied due to their end-to-end characteristics, they still face challenges such as missed cell detections, image degradation, and insufficient feature utilization when processing multi-modal cell microscopy images, resulting in limited segmentation accuracy. To address these issues, this paper proposes a novel deep learning framework designed to achieve accurate and efficient segmentation of multi-modal cell microscopy images without the need for manual parameter tuning or algorithm switching. The framework enhances segmentation accuracy through three core modules: the Weighted Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN) reduces missed cell detections in low-contrast regions by constructing a weighted bidirectional cross-scale connection mechanism; Frequency-aware Feature Fusion (FreqFusion) enables precise identification of cell boundaries under complex degradation conditions via a frequency-domain adaptive mechanism; and the Mixed Local Channel Attention (MLCA) guides the model to focus on key channels and difficult-to-segment regions. On a self-built dataset, the framework achieved an average precision of 95.07%, a cell detection rate of 96.72%, and a segmentation speed of 59.23 frames per second (FPS), with its robust generalization ability further verified on public datasets. These advancements lay a solid foundation for the quantitative analysis of microscopic images in precision medicine.
